So much of what you do every day generates data — your loyalty card, your apps, your groceries. And once it’s collected, it doesn’t stay with the company you gave it to. Data brokers buy it and combine it into a profile of you. Your data is their product — and you’ve never even heard of them.

From that data, brokers generate thousands of inferences about you: your income, your housing, even your health. These inferences are guesses, and they’re often wrong — but they’re packaged and sold as fact.

The companies you shop with sell your information to data brokers — who resell your profile to insurers setting your rates, retailers deciding what price you see at checkout, and government agencies surveilling your neighbors. ICE has bought data broker profiles to access personal and location information it would otherwise need a warrant to obtain.

What do they know about you? Is it even correct? You have no way to know — unless you request your data.

Get Your Data Take Action

Data brokers are companies that collect, aggregate, and sell personal information about you even if you don’t have a direct relationship with them. Data brokers compile this data into a detailed profile that then gets sold to retailers, insurance companies, even to the government. Here's something they don't advertise: in a growing number of states, you have the legal right to see the data companies hold on you, ask them to delete it, and tell them to stop selling it. You just have to ask and we can show you how.

One way that we've seen our data be weaponized against us is when companies set prices we see at check out based on what they think they know about our habits, or predilections, or our bank accounts. This is practice is called Surveillance Pricing, and Gen-Z for Change and Consumer Reports are working hard to make it not just creepy, but also against the law.
